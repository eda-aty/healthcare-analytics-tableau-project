# 🏥 Healthcare Analytics – Treatment Effectiveness & Cost Efficiency

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes patient outcomes, treatment effectiveness, healthcare costs, and seasonal demand patterns using interactive **Tableau dashboards**. The goal is to support hospital leadership and clinical teams in identifying **high-value treatments**, understanding **cost drivers**, and improving **patient recovery outcomes** through data-driven insights.

The analysis focuses on answering key strategic questions such as:

* Which treatments deliver the best recovery outcomes relative to their cost?
* Which diagnoses drive the highest patient volume and treatment cost?
* Does higher treatment cost translate into better patient outcomes?
* How do patient demand and healthcare costs change seasonally?

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

Hospital leadership need to understand which diagnoses and treatment types drive patient volume, healthcare costs, and recovery outcomes in order to improve treatment efficiency and allocate resources more effectively.

## 💡 Key Insights

- Overall recovery rate remains moderate (~25%), indicating significant opportunity to improve treatment effectiveness without proportionally increasing cost.

- Older age groups (especially 65+) account for higher patient volume and higher average treatment costs, making them a key driver of overall healthcare spend.

- High patient volume does not always translate into the highest total cost; chronic and complex conditions such as Heart Disease generate disproportionately high costs despite lower patient counts.

- Cost vs. volume analysis shows that diagnoses like Heart Disease and Hypertension are more expensive per patient and are also associated with higher failure or no-change rates.

- Treatment effectiveness analysis demonstrates that higher-cost treatments do not consistently achieve better recovery outcomes.

- Rehabilitation and Monitoring treatments deliver the highest recovery rates while also achieving the lowest cost per successful recovery, indicating strong cost-effectiveness.

- Medication and Surgery account for the highest average treatment costs but do not consistently outperform lower-cost treatments in recovery outcomes.

- Seasonal analysis reveals predictable demand peaks during January, July, and August, with Spring and Summer driving the highest total treatment costs.

- Seasonal diagnosis patterns suggest that conditions such as Flu, Asthma, and Hypertension exhibit clear seasonal spikes, supporting proactive capacity and resource planning.
